# Play invitations

These original activity suggestions apply the broad principles of responsive, playful interaction. They have not been individually validated as treatments or as a way to accelerate milestones. [WHO early-development guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/97892400020986) and [AAP play guidance](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/the-power-of-play-how-fun-and-games-help-children-thrive.aspx) explain the foundation.

Use across 15–24 months according to current ability. Stay with Braden, begin simply, and stop when he turns away, becomes tired, or dislikes it. The aim is participation and enjoyment.

## STEAM: water transfer

**Materials:** two large cups and a stable basin with a little water.

**Try:** show pouring once, hand him a cup, and describe what he does: “In. Out. Full. Empty.” Let him repeat.

**Simpler:** you steady his cup while he tips it. **Extension:** offer two differently sized cups; explore without testing predictions.

**Observe:** what draws his interest and how he asks for another turn. **Safety:** continuous adult attention within arm's reach; empty immediately afterward. See [water rules](playroom.md).

## STEAM: build and change

**Materials:** large toddler blocks on a clear floor.

**Try:** place blocks together, then let him take over. Describe changes such as “It fell” or “This one is underneath.”

**Simpler:** put one block in a large container. **Extension:** arrange a wide base or a short wall together.

**Observe:** his experiments, not tower height. **Safety:** no small pieces or heavy blocks stacked overhead.

At this age STEAM means exploring materials, cause and effect, construction, art, and everyday size/quantity language. Technology can mean a simple safe tool, such as a scoop; no app or device is needed.

## Art: marks and water painting

**Materials:** large age-appropriate crayons and secured paper, or a sturdy brush with water on a washable surface.

**Try:** make a mark, then give him space to explore. Talk about the action without asking him to copy a shape.

**Simpler:** adult holds the paper steady. **Extension:** offer a second tool or color, one change at a time.

**Observe:** what movements or materials he enjoys. **Safety:** supervise mouthing, remove broken pieces, and follow product age labels. Use washable paint only when appropriate for the product and his behavior.

## Reading: follow his attention

**Materials:** a favorite board book.

**Try:** let him choose a page; name what he notices and wait for a response. Rereading is welcome. Use the languages you naturally speak with him.

**Simpler:** enjoy one picture together. **Extension:** connect the picture to an object or action nearby.

**Observe:** looks, gestures, sounds, and words. **Safety:** choose sturdy books and remove damaged loose parts. There is no requirement to finish a book.

## Sports foundations: ball and travel

**Materials:** large soft ball and clear space.

**Try:** sit and roll the ball toward each other. If walking comfortably, he can carry it to a basket.

**Simpler:** bring it within reach while seated. **Extension:** invite a gentle kick or a walk around a low floor marker if appropriate to his skills.

**Observe:** enjoyment and how much help he needs. **Safety:** keep away from stairs and slippery surfaces. Offer free movement, not formal sports training.

## Music: copy and pause

**Materials:** your voice, hands, or a sturdy age-rated toddler drum.

**Try:** sing a familiar song, copy his beat, or pause and leave room for him to respond.

**Simpler:** sway together or listen briefly. **Extension:** vary fast/slow or start/stop as a game without expecting reliable rule following.

**Observe:** whether he wants to listen, move, vocalize, or stop. **Safety:** comfortable volume, no small detachable instrument parts or homemade loose-fill shakers. Music is for shared enjoyment; this plan makes no IQ or language-acceleration claims.
