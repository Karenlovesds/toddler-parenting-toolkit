# Confidence grows through everyday connection

WHO recommends responsive caregiving and early learning opportunities in the first three years. Its evidence review supports the broad approach, not a guarantee that any one activity creates confidence. [WHO guideline](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/97892400020986)

## A useful daily pattern

Notice what the child is looking at or trying. Join them, use a few words, and pause for their look, sound, gesture, or action. Offer enough help to make participation possible. Let them refuse a game or return to something familiar.

Practical scripts, adapted for your family:

- When they try: “You kept turning it. It fits now.”
- When they want help: “That is tricky. I can hold this part.”
- When they are upset: “You wanted more water. You're upset. I'm here.”
- When they hit: gently stop the action. “I won't let you hit. Gentle hands.” Redirect and help them settle.
- At a transition: “One more page, then pajamas.” Follow through calmly.
- For autonomy: “This book or that book?” Offer two choices you can accept.

Avoid pressure to perform for visitors, labels such as “bad child,” and comparisons with other children. Being confident can include approaching slowly, seeking reassurance, and asking for help. The child does not need to be outgoing or happy all the time.

Use consistent limits, demonstrate what to do, and notice helpful behavior. Avoid hitting, shaming, or yelling as discipline. Hunger and tiredness can make behavior harder. These principles follow [AAP positive discipline guidance](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Disciplining-Your-Child.aspx).

After a difficult moment, reconnect. If you become overwhelmed, place them safely and get support from another caregiver when possible. The family plan should be sustainable for you too.
