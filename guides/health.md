# Health and doctor preparation

This guide is educational. The child's clinician should individualize care using their history, growth, and examination.

## Everyday foundations

- Follow a predictable meal/snack rhythm and offer variety. Three meals and two or three planned snacks is one common toddler pattern; coordinate across caregivers rather than duplicating meals. [AAP feeding guidance](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/nutrition/Pages/Making-Sure-Your-Child-is-Eating-Enough.aspx)
- Prepare food for their chewing skills. Keep them seated upright and watch while they eat. Avoid whole nuts, popcorn, whole grapes, and chunks of hard raw vegetables; modify shape and texture appropriately. Avoid eating while walking or playing. [CDC choking prevention](https://www.cdc.gov/infant-toddler-nutrition/foods-and-drinks/choking-hazards.html)
- Brush twice daily using a rice-grain-sized smear of fluoride toothpaste for children under three, with adult help. Discuss dental care with their dentist. [AAP fluoride guidance](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/oral-health/Pages/FAQ-Fluoride-and-Children.aspx)
- Use the [routine guide](routine.md) for sleep and movement. Record persistent concerns rather than trying to optimize every day.

At 12 months, discuss milk or formula transitions, continued breastfeeding if desired, vitamin D, food allergies, and any growth concerns with the child's clinician. CDC says plain pasteurized whole cow's milk or a suitable fortified unsweetened dairy alternative can be introduced from 12 months; individual needs vary. Use the [illustrated food guide](food-safety.md) for preparation and choking prevention.

## Visits and screening

For US care, CDC reports the AAP recommendation for general developmental screening at 9, 18, and 30 months and autism-specific screening at 18 and 24 months. A screening result is not a diagnosis. Confirm the appropriate visit and screening schedule with your clinic if outside the US. [CDC screening guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/autism/hcp/diagnosis/screening.html)

At the next visit, bring your questions about communication, hearing, movement, sleep, feeding, teeth, and growth. Ask the clinician to review vaccinations and any locally indicated screening using current guidance and the child's records. This repository deliberately does not prescribe a vaccine schedule, supplements, or medication doses without that information.

## When to raise developmental concerns

If the child loses a skill, is missing an age checkpoint, or you are concerned about how they hear, communicate, move, or relate, contact their clinician promptly rather than waiting for the next birthday. In the US, you can also contact early intervention directly. [CDC action guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/1-year.html)

This is not an illness-triage guide. Follow your clinic's advice for symptoms and urgent care; use your local emergency service for an emergency.

## Recording information safely

Copy the [doctor note](../templates/doctor-note.md) into the ignored `private/` folder. Keep parent observations separate from clinician findings. Record instructions exactly, including follow-up dates. For any prescribed medicine, copy the clinician/pharmacist-confirmed medicine, concentration, dose, route, and frequency rather than calculating from this guide.

Use the [growth log](../templates/growth-log.md) for dated measurements and clinician interpretation. A single measurement or percentile does not determine health. Do not turn growth tracking into a target to gain or lose weight.
